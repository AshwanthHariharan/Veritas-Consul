AI Governance in the Age of Agentic AI

January 2026 AI Governance 8 min read

Artificial intelligence is rapidly moving beyond systems that simply respond to human instructions. The emergence of agentic AI represents a significant shift. AI systems are increasingly capable of planning tasks, making decisions, interacting with other systems, using tools, adapting to changing circumstances and executing actions with limited human intervention.

This creates enormous opportunities for organisations.

It also creates a new governance challenge.

When an AI system is capable of acting rather than merely advising, organisations need to ask a different set of questions: Who is accountable for the AI's decisions? What controls govern its actions? How do we know what the AI has done? What happens when it makes an unexpected decision? When should a human intervene?

These are no longer purely technology questions. They are questions of governance, risk, accountability and organisational responsibility.

What is Agentic AI?

Traditional AI applications generally operate within a relatively defined interaction. A user provides an input. The system processes it. The system produces an output.

Agentic AI introduces another dimension: autonomous action.

An agent may interpret an objective, determine a sequence of tasks, interact with tools or applications, evaluate results and take further action.

For example, an AI agent within an organisation could potentially:

  • Analyse customer requests
  • Retrieve information from internal systems
  • Draft and send communications
  • Initiate workflows
  • Make recommendations
  • Interact with software applications
  • Monitor events
  • Escalate issues
  • Execute predefined business processes

The more autonomy an AI system receives, the more important its governance becomes.

The Veritas Perspective

At Veritas Consulting, we believe that AI governance should be designed around the organisation's actual use of AI.

A policy written in isolation will not be enough.

Governance needs to reflect:

  • What AI systems the organisation uses
  • What decisions they influence
  • What data they access
  • What actions they can perform
  • How much autonomy they have
  • What risks they introduce
  • What controls already exist
  • Where human oversight is required

Our approach is therefore practical and risk-based.

We help organisations understand their current AI environment, identify governance gaps and establish a structured roadmap for responsible and controlled AI adoption.
