GRC & Technology Risk

January 2026 GRC 7 min read

Governance, Risk and Compliance should not operate as separate functions.

An organisation may have policies, controls, audits and compliance programmes — yet still struggle to understand its overall risk exposure.

The reason is often fragmentation.

Technology risk sits with IT. Compliance sits with another function. Vendor risk is managed elsewhere. Internal controls may exist without a clear connection to business objectives.

GRC brings these elements together.

At Veritas Consulting, we help organisations establish a structured approach to governance, risk and compliance while addressing the technology risks that increasingly sit at the centre of modern business.

What Does GRC Mean?

Governance establishes direction, accountability and oversight.

Risk management helps organisations understand uncertainty and make informed decisions.

Compliance ensures that applicable obligations are identified, understood and addressed.

Together, they create a framework through which management can make decisions with greater confidence.

Our GRC & Technology Risk Services

Governance Advisory
We help organisations strengthen governance structures, accountability models, decision-making processes and oversight mechanisms. The objective is to ensure that governance is connected to business strategy rather than existing only as a policy framework.

Risk Management
We help organisations establish practical risk-management approaches covering:

  • Enterprise risks
  • Operational risks
  • Technology risks
  • Information-security risks
  • Third-party risks
  • Compliance risks
  • Business continuity risks
  • Emerging risks

We help management move from risk registers to risk-informed decision-making.

Technology Risk
Technology has become inseparable from business operations. Cloud platforms, SaaS applications, AI systems, cybersecurity, infrastructure, software development and digital services all introduce new dimensions of risk. Veritas helps organisations understand and assess technology-related risks and establish appropriate controls.

Compliance Advisory
Regulatory and contractual obligations can become difficult to manage as organisations grow. We help organisations identify applicable requirements, map obligations to controls and establish mechanisms for monitoring compliance.

Internal Audits
We provide internal audit support across management systems, compliance frameworks and organisational control environments. The objective is to provide management with an independent perspective on the effectiveness of existing controls.

Third-Party / Vendor Risk
Organisations increasingly depend on external service providers. A vendor may process sensitive information, operate critical infrastructure or support important business processes. We help organisations establish structured third-party risk practices covering:

  • Vendor due diligence
  • Risk classification
  • Security and compliance assessments
  • Contractual requirements
  • Control reviews
  • Periodic monitoring
  • Remediation

Policy, Procedure & Control Framework Development
Policies should define organisational intent. Procedures should explain how that intent is operationalised. Controls should provide evidence that the required activities are actually happening. Veritas helps organisations connect these three layers into a coherent control environment.

How Veritas Approaches GRC

We believe GRC should not create another layer of bureaucracy.

Instead, it should help management answer five fundamental questions:

What matters to us?

Understanding organisational priorities

What can go wrong?

Identifying potential risks

What are we doing about it?

Establishing controls

How do we know our controls are working?

Monitoring and testing

What needs to improve?

Continual improvement

That is the foundation of practical GRC.

Why Veritas?

Veritas brings together management systems, technology risk, audit, compliance and governance rather than looking at each area in isolation.

This allows us to help organisations develop a more connected view of risk and control.
